Understanding cricket beyond the scorecard: who delivers when the game is on the line.
Pressure is the difficulty of achieving a match objective with the resources remaining. We track that difficulty on every delivery, measure how much each player improves their team's position compared with a typical peer, and check whether they do it again and again.
Submitted by Devesh Chaudhary · IPL + men's T20I · performance window 2025–26
01

What is pressure?

Every team has an objective (post a defendable total, chase a target, or stop the opposition) and finite resources: runs, wickets, deliveries, set batters and bowling options. Pressure is how hard the objective is with what is left.

How the index reads a match

Each delivery gets a 0–10 pressure reading for the batting side, computed before the ball is bowled. It rises when the required rate climbs, wickets fall or time runs out, and when the stakes are higher (knockouts).

Average index by over across all 2025–26 deliveries. Pressure is not only a death-overs story: the powerplay carries as much, and late overs spike only in close games. That is why we read the state, not the over number.

TakeawayThe event alone never decides the reading. The state of the match does.
02

Our approach

Six steps turn raw ball-by-ball data into a ranking. The key idea: a player is credited only for the pressure they personally relieved compared with what a typical player would have done in the exact same moment.

Rebuild

Reconstruct the match state before every ball: score, target, balls, wickets.

✓ No future leakage

Read context

Attach the 190 context factors: partnership, phase, matchup, venue, stakes.

✓ Missing-data flags

Pressure index

Score batting- and bowling-team pressure 0–10 on every delivery.

✓ Calibrated by phase

Peer expectation

What does a typical player in this role do in this exact state?

✓ Role + phase baseline

Relief

Compare pressure after the actual ball with pressure after the peer ball.

✓ Only own actions

Reliability

How often does the player win pressure episodes? Shrunk for small samples.

✓ Consistency, not one-offs
Δ = Ppeer − PactualPer delivery: positive means the player relieved more pressure than a peer would
Relief % = 100 · ΣΔ ⁄ Σ Pepisode startSummed over distinct pressure episodes, so long innings aren't over-penalised
DNA = Relief % × Reliability %How much pressure they relieve × how often they do it
Why reliability matters

5 rescues from 7 chances looks better than 14 from 19, but the smaller record is far less certain. Reliability = 100·s ⁄ (n + 6) pulls small samples toward caution.

Worked example · rank 1 batter
18/37→41.9%×52%=21.77

Tilak Varma: 18 successful pressure episodes from 37 gives reliability 100·18 ⁄ 43 = 41.9%. Multiplied by 52% extra relief, his DNA Rating is 21.77.

A partner's wicket is a team event, never a debit to the other batter. Bowlers are only credited for their own wickets.

04

How we annotate pressure

Historical deliveries (2022–2024) are labelled by a review workflow, and the model learns from those labels. Agents propose; cricket analysts decide.

The workflow
1 · Reconstruct
Pre-ball resource snapshot, replayed against scorecards.
data
2 · 50 agent review streams
Score resources, chase feasibility, partnerships, matchups and stakes each propose a rating and reasons.
agents
3 · Analyst adjudication
Cricket analysts resolve disagreements and rate both teams on a 0–5 scale, rationale kept.
humans
4 · Train and calibrate
P = 10 · g(context), learned from labels and validated on held-out seasons and matches.
model
The 0–5 annotation scale
0–1 · CalmOver 4, 38/0 chasing 150. Any outcome barely matters.
2 · BuildingOver 12, 92/3 needing 9 an over. Dots start to hurt.
3 · TenseTwo wickets in an over, new batter facing a strike bowler.
4–5 · SevereFinal over, 12 to defend, set finisher on strike, knockout game.
TakeawayLabels come from cricket judgement, weights come from data, and trust comes from held-out validation.
